# Sonargaon: historical city and archaeological area

Source: Banglapedia, “Sonargaon,” published by the Asiatic Society of Bangladesh. The wider Sonargaon region has a long history, but this source does not establish one exact founding date for the whole city or archaeological area.

Sonargaon, also called Suvarnagrama in historical accounts, is a historic region in present-day Narayanganj. Banglapedia traces its political importance to at least the sixth century CE, when the area was associated with the Vanga or Samatata polities. This does not establish the age of every surviving ruin or building. The remains are spread across the area and belong to different periods; there is no single intact ancient city plan that can be assigned one founding date.

Sonargaon’s clearest period of political prominence was medieval. In 1338 it became the capital of the independent Sultanate founded by Fakhruddin Mubarak Shah, and it remained the capital until 1352. Later it served at different times as a provincial centre and as the base of Isa Khan’s Bhati kingdom. Ibn Battuta described Sonargaon as an important port city with trade links to China, Java, and the Maldives. Its surrounding region became known for fine muslin textiles.

Sonargaon lost political prominence as Mughal power grew and Dhaka became the provincial capital. The surviving heritage landscape includes medieval Sultanate and Mughal remains, as well as colonial-era buildings. Panam Nagar is a distinct later merchant town within the wider Sonargaon area; its surviving houses are from the colonial period, especially the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries. Goaldi Mosque is another distinct monument in the area, dated to 1519.

Do not confuse Sonargaon with Wari-Bateshwar in Narsingdi. They are separate archaeological places with different histories; the roughly 2,500-year-old fortified-city claim belongs to Wari-Bateshwar, not to Sonargaon as a whole.
