# Sreemangal and the history of tea cultivation in Bangladesh

The Bangladesh Tea Board's history says experimental tea cultivation began in 1840 at the Pioneer Garden near the Chittagong Club. Mr Sconce, then Collector of Chittagong, brought tea seeds from Assam and Chinese tea plants from the Calcutta Botanical Garden for that planting. This was in Chittagong, not Sreemangal.

In the Sylhet region, the Malnichhera Tea Estate was established in 1854. Sources distinguish the garden's establishment from the start of commercial production: the Bangladesh Tea Association dates commercial production there to 1857. This is the early tea-industry date for Sylhet; it should not be presented as the opening date of a Sreemangal estate. Tea planting later spread across the hills and valleys of Greater Sylhet, including the Balisera Valley around Sreemangal. The sources consulted do not identify one person or a precise year for the first tea garden in Sreemangal itself.

British-owned plantation companies organized tea as a commercial crop for regional and export markets. Around Sreemangal, the estates became a major source of wage work in cultivation and processing and supported transport, trade, and later tea auction activity. The Bangladesh Tea Association describes the wider industry as supporting estate jobs as well as trading, brokerage, warehousing, and transport. The Bangladesh Tea Research Institute was established near Sreemangal in 1957, adding research and technical work to the local tea economy.

This economic change also had a serious human cost. Plantation owners recruited workers from other parts of the subcontinent, and tea-worker communities became a lasting part of the region. The International Labour Organization's study documents continuing problems in tea plantation workers' living and working conditions. Describe the industry as both economically important and shaped by unequal colonial-era labour relations; do not imply that plantation growth benefited all local people equally.
