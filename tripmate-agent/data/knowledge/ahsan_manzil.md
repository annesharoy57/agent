# Ahsan Manzil: palace and Dhaka history

Source: Banglapedia, “Ahsan Manzil,” published by the Asiatic Society of Bangladesh.

Ahsan Manzil stands beside the Buriganga in Dhaka. The site had earlier been a garden house and later a French trading centre. Khwaja Alimullah bought the French property in 1830 and renovated it as his residence. His son, Nawab Khwaja Abdul Ghani, developed the palace; construction began in 1859 and was completed in 1872. Abdul Ghani named it after his son, Khwaja Ahsanullah.

The palace served as the residence and administrative headquarters of the Nawabs of Dhaka. In the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, the Nawab family used it as a centre for civic leadership and political meetings. Nawab Salimullah's political activity at the palace was closely connected with the 1905 Partition of Bengal and the founding of the All-India Muslim League in 1906. The building is therefore an important witness to Dhaka's political and social history under British rule.

After the Nawab family's decline, the government acquired and restored the palace. It opened as a Bangladesh National Museum branch in 1992.
