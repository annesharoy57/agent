# Mahasthangarh and Ancient Pundranagara

Source: UNESCO World Heritage Centre Tentative List submission, “Cultural Landscape of Mahasthan and Karatoya River.” This note paraphrases the State Party submission; inclusion on a Tentative List is not the same as inscription on the World Heritage List.

Mahasthangarh is the fortified citadel of an ancient urban centre identified in historical sources as Pundranagara, in the region of Pundravardhana, near present-day Bogura. The UNESCO Tentative List submission dates the urban centre to around the 4th–3rd century BCE, making it roughly 2,300–2,400 years old. Archaeological evidence indicates that its first fortification was built of mud during the Mauryan period. The city remained active through later periods, including Gupta, Pala, and Sena rule.

The site matters because it preserves evidence of early urban life and administration in Bengal. A fragmentary stone inscription in Brahmi script refers to Pundranagara and records an instruction to an administrator to help people facing hardship during a calamity. This is an unusually vivid glimpse of public administration and relief in ancient Bengal.

The citadel stands beside the Karatoya River and is surrounded by ramparts. The wider cultural landscape includes archaeological mounds, old tanks, and remains of Buddhist monasteries, Brahmanical temples, mosques, and shrines. Excavations and dating show that the settlement changed over many centuries rather than belonging to only one dynasty or religion.

The UNESCO source describes Mahasthangarh as part of a proposed cultural landscape on Bangladesh’s Tentative List, not as an already inscribed UNESCO World Heritage Site. This note does not verify current visiting conditions or opening hours.
