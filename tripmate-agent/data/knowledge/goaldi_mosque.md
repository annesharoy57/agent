# Goaldi Mosque: a Sultanate-era monument

Goaldi Mosque is a small historic mosque in Sonargaon. It dates to 1519, during the reign of Sultan Alauddin Husain Shah. An inscription identified Mullah Hizabar Akbar Khan as its builder. It is about 507 years old in 2026 and is one of the surviving reminders of Sonargaon's Sultanate-era history.

The mosque was built as a place for local Muslims to offer prayers. It has a square prayer chamber, originally covered by a single dome, and four corner towers. Three mihrabs (prayer niches) line the west wall, matching the three arched entrances on the east. The larger central mihrab has carved dark stone decoration, while the side mihrabs have terracotta patterns. The red brick exterior also has terracotta decoration.

The mosque is an example of Bengal's pre-Mughal, Sultanate-era architecture. Its enclosed square prayer hall, single dome, and circular corner towers are characteristic of a regional mosque design. The entrance facade has three arched doorways; smaller doorways also open on the north and south sides. The Daily Star describes the walls transitioning to an octagonal upper section beneath the dome and the four corner towers as three-layered circular bastions.

After falling into ruin, the mosque was restored by the Department of Archaeology in 1975.

- The mosque has a square plan and was originally covered by a single dome.
- It has four corner towers and three mihrabs; the central mihrab is larger and decorated with carved dark stone, while the side mihrabs have terracotta patterns.
