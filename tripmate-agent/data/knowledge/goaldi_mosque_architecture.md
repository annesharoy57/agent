# Goaldi Mosque architecture

Goaldi Mosque in Sonargaon is an early sixteenth-century, pre-Mughal example of Bengal's enclosed-square mosque type. Its square prayer chamber is capped by one dome. Squinch arches at the four corners help the square chamber transition to the dome, with pendentives supporting it.

Four circular corner towers distinguish the mosque from the more usual octagonal corner towers. Three arched entrances open on the east. The west wall has three mihrabs: the larger central niche is decorated with carved black basalt and floral and arabesque patterns; the side niches use brick and terracotta. Terracotta patterns also decorate the brick exterior.

These features combine a regional square-and-dome plan with skilled brick, terracotta, and stone decoration. The mosque was carefully restored in 1975 after falling into ruin.
